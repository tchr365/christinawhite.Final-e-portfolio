Video Production

Traditional and AI Video Production Projects

TRADITIONAL VIDEO

This traditional educational video is titled "How Bees Help Plants Grow: The Power of Pollination." To produce this video, the first step I took was to determine the video topic. I love bees and plants, so I thought that this would be an enjoyable topic to work on for an educational video. Then, I created a storyboard for the video, including narration and types of pictures that I would need. After that, I went to Pexels.com to find the free pictures that I needed to match my narration script. I made sure to record the source information for each. I also found some free music that would go well with the bee theme and noted that source information as well. I used iMovie on an iPad to create the video. I had never used this application before, so this was a learning curve for me. I added all the pictures that I wanted to use and made sure they were in the correct order. Then I added the music file. To add the narration, I used the microphone to record myself reading the narration script. Once this was complete, I listened to the narration and adjusted the lengths of all the picture timings. I also made sure the volume of the background music was at an appropriate level so as not to take away from the narration. A formal title was added to the first slide, and captions were added to a few of the other slides. I enabled some camera and transition effects on different pictures to fit the narration. I did not see where I could add any empty frames in the iMovie app, so I typed out the credits in a Google Doc. Then I snipped them and saved them as PNG files. These were uploaded and added to the end of the video. Finally, I faded out the background music at the end of the video. I was able to upload the completed video directly to my YouTube account from the iMovie application.

AI VIDEO

This AI educational video is titled "How Bees Help Plants Grow: The Power of Pollination." Using ChatGPT, I prompted it for the script, storyboard, and accompanying photos. I also asked it to generate the voice narration, but it told me that feature was not enabled. I did a Google search for AI voice generators and saw that Canva had one, so I tried it. I uploaded the script and listened to it. The voice generation did not sound right, so that was not going to be an option. I finally found a voice on Google AI Studio that worked well for this video. I was not happy with the background music that ChatGPT created, so I used the music from my traditional video. I began to create the video on iMovie, adding the pictures, titles, captions, effects, and then the sound files. This is where an issue occurred. I learned quickly that you cannot layer two MP3 files on the mobile iMovie app. When there is one already added and you try to add another one, it deletes the other one and replaces it. I tried to use Adobe to add the music track, and it did not work the way I needed it to. The video was very small in the middle of a white page. At this point, the only workaround that I could think of was to use the microphone on the iMovie app to voice-record the AI narrator file as it played on my computer. It does not sound as crisp as the original file, but it worked. This is the only way I could get the layered voice and music on the iPad iMovie application to work. I learned that it is important to know in advance what the limitations are of applications and platforms you are using so that you have backup options available to complete the project properly.
